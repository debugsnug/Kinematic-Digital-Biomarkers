RECORDING-SITE / ACQUISITION CONFOUND - WHAT IS AND IS NOT ESTABLISHED
- The dataset documentation (README, paper) states participants were recruited at laboratories in Belgium and the Netherlands and that each laboratory trained its own DeepLabCut model (federated processing). It does NOT list which child was recorded where, nor any camera/calibration/session variable. The statement 'all TD in Belgium, 6 DCP in Amsterdam' that appeared in earlier project text is NOT verifiable from the supplied documents and has been removed.
- Subject numbers are strongly structured by group (blocks 25-42 and 67-89 contain only DCP; TD occupy 1-2 and 43-66); a classifier given only the subject number reaches balanced accuracy 0.85. Case numbers are therefore a proxy for recruitment/processing batch and must never be a predictor.
- Best balanced accuracy of each acquisition proxy (max over classifiers; 10 partitions): apparent_body_size 0.60; camera_frame_positions 0.67; clip_length 0.76; subject_number_only 0.85. Clip length (frames per repetition) is the strongest non-ID proxy; movement_duration_s equals clip length (step 37), so clinical slowness and recording/trimming differences cannot be separated. Duration-excluded ablations are in results/feature_sets.
- Excel metadata and filenames contain no site information.
- Group is also unbalanced on arm side (TD 18 left / 3 right) and, per the paper, sex (DCP 10 F / 20 M; TD 16 F / 5 M). Left-arm videos were mirrored to look like right-arm videos.
- CONCLUSION: group and acquisition batch cannot be separated with the supplied data. High accuracy may partly reflect recording setup. Validation on an independent, site-balanced cohort is required before any clinical claim.
